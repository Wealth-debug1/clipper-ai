We are one week into the high-profile trial between Elon Musk and OpenAI. Here's where things stand. Elon Musk, the world's richest man, was a co-founder of OpenAI. He and the now CEO Sam Altman were friends a decade ago, and both were in the Oakland courthouse this week as we started to get two sides to this story. At the heart of this case, whether Altman betrayed OpenAI's founding promise to build the company as a nonprofit, quote, for the benefit of all humanity. Musk accuses Altman and President Greg Brockman of tricking him using his funding and used their prestige to build an $800 billion company they now allegedly profit from personally. The lawsuit has been whittled down to two claims: breach of charitable trust and unjust enrichment. Here's what we heard inside the courtroom. Musk on the stand this week claimed that they stole a charity and said its current structure with a lucrative for-profit and multibillion-dollar investment from Microsoft violate that original mission. He also took credit for building OpenAI from the ground up, saying that he could have started a for-profit company but chose not to. Quote, I came up with the idea, the name, recruited the key people, taught them everything I know, and provided all of the initial funding. Musk said safety was paramount in those early days, telling the court he wanted OpenAI to make society look more like Star Trek than The Terminator. Google was also a central player in this case. Musk said the reason OpenAI exists is because Google was dominating the AI space after acquiring DeepMind. He said, He was motivated by his perception that Larry Page, Google's co-founder, was not concerned about AI safety. Page, according to Musk, accused him of being too pro-human, also known as speciesist. Musk pointed to one moment as the turning point when OpenAI removed its internal profit caps and then accepted a $10 billion investment from Microsoft. That, Musk says, is when they, quote, stole the charity. Things in court got combative. Musk accused the opposing counsel of lying and misleading the jury. William Savitt, the lawyer for OpenAI, repeatedly told Musk to just answer the simple yes or no questions. Musk fired back with, "Your questions are not simple. They're designed to trick me." OpenAI has tried to poke holes in Musk's narrative. Their defense boils down to Musk's attempts to control the AI company. Altman's lawyers argue that Musk knew about and at times supported a for-profit structure and is now using the courts to attack a rival while building his own competing AI company XAI. Musk is seeking $134 billion in damages, which he says he'll donate to the OpenAI charity if he wins. He wants Altman and Brockman removed from leadership. Plus, he's seeking to unwind OpenAI's recent restructuring. All of these moves could throw a wrench in the company's highly anticipated IPO. The trial has an advisory jury, which means Judge Gonzalez Rogers will make the final call on both liability and remedies. Still to come, we are expecting to hear from Sam Altman, Greg Brockman, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, and some key early OpenAI employees over the next 2 weeks.
